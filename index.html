AI in Workforce: Human Labor vs. AI Automation

Zero-Sum or Positive-Sum?

Conference affiliation: BigData 2026 Workshop
Date: December 14, 2026 (morning) | Location: Sheraton Phoenix Downtown, Phoenix, Arizona, USA

This workshop builds a multidisciplinary community around one core question: how should AI transform work so that human capability, dignity, and productivity are expanded rather than displaced. The program brings together machine learning, economics, public policy, and HCI perspectives through invited talks, papers, posters, panels, and networking sessions.

Overview

AI4Workforce examines feedback loops between AI advancement and labor market dynamics, moving beyond a binary replacement narrative. The workshop targets practical and theoretical questions about task decomposition, augmentation design, automation boundaries, and new skill formation in the AI era.

A central theme is positive-sum design: identifying technical, economic, and governance mechanisms that allow AI systems to improve human work outcomes while reducing bias, preserving agency, and supporting equitable access across regions and sectors.

Anticipated audience: 100-200 attendees, including researchers and practitioners from machine learning, economics, public policy, HCI, and industry.

Call for Papers

Topics of Interest

  • Foundational Models and the Nature of Work: modeling jobs as tasks/capabilities, AI-enabled workflows, and emerging role/skill patterns.
  • Augmentation vs. Replacement: frameworks for deciding substitution vs. collaboration, and metrics for automation potential.
  • AI in Labor Markets: hiring, evaluation, algorithmic management, fairness, and workforce representation learning.
  • Designing Positive-Sum Outcomes: dignity-preserving automation, reskilling pathways, and human-AI copilot models.
  • Long-Term Societal Impact and Safety: inequality, institutions, governance, labor dignity, and systemic risk mitigation.
